Sky Atlas privacy policy

Last updated: October 20, 2026

This policy covers the Sky Atlas app for iPhone, made by Maksym Motorny.

What the app collects

Nothing. Sky Atlas has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it makes no network requests: nothing you do in the app leaves your iPhone through it.

Your location

If you allow it, Sky Atlas uses your iPhone’s approximate location, not its precise one, to show the sky as seen from where you are. The app uses it on your iPhone only and never sends it anywhere. It keeps the last location it got on your iPhone, so that it can show your sky right away the next time you open it. Like other app data, that location is part of your iPhone’s backups, including iCloud Backup if you use it, which Apple handles under its own privacy policy.

You can allow or deny location access when the app asks, and change it at any time in Settings → Apps → Sky Atlas → Location. Without it, the app shows the sky from the last location it knew or from San Francisco. Deleting the app deletes the location it kept.

TestFlight

If you test a beta version through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple collects crash logs, usage data (such as the number of sessions and crashes) and the feedback you send, with details about your device, and shares them with us; if you were invited by email, they come with the name and email address we invited you with. We use them only to fix and improve Sky Atlas and don’t share them with anyone; write to us to have what we got about you deleted. Apple’s TestFlight & Privacy page explains what it collects and how long it keeps it: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/test-flight/

Changes and contact

If Sky Atlas starts handling data differently, this policy will say so before that version is released, with a new date above. Questions: mmotorny@gmail.com.
